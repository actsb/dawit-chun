OMY × 2026 SOTA — Benchmarking Frontier VLA & World Models on a Real Arm 🤖 flagship work

Five state-of-the-art policies, one 6-DoF OpenManipulator-Y, and every engineering problem between a checkpoint and a working robot

I designed the task, collected the data (100 teleop episodes, 3 cameras), fine-tuned five 2026 frontier models on H100/A100 cloud GPUs, and evaluated them on the physical arm: GR00T N1.7 (NVIDIA), MolmoAct2 (Ai2), VLA-JEPA, FastWAM, and LingBot-VA.

  • Best model: GR00T N1.7 at ~80% success — then I attacked its failure modes: start-stop hesitation, off-workspace attention, chunk-seam jitter
  • ROI token pruning: cut image-patch tokens outside the fixed workspace — attention cleaned up at ≤10 ms added latency (keep-mask built once, cached)
  • Trajectory smoothing: Savitzky-Golay seam filters between action chunks, age-weighted smooth-chunk ensembling, RTC-logic fixes, sync/async action queues
  • Technical story + inference pipeline contributed upstream — PR to ROBOTIS-GIT/docs (feature-omy-vlamodels-inference)

Prior-ACT — Scene-Prior Imitation Learning on OMX

A newly developed ACT variant: a frozen DINOv2 scene prior seeds the CVAE latent, steering the policy before it acts

On a drawer-manipulation study (OpenManipulator-X, 150 demonstrations, three drawers), I built and compared new policy architectures against vanilla ACT:

  • Prior-ACT (mine): DINOv2 looks at the image and predicts the CVAE latent that the action encoder would have produced — a scene-seeded prior at inference. Replay MAE 1.45–1.93% of joint range vs. 3.22% for vanilla ACT
  • MoE-Diffusion: hierarchical diffusion with phase-adaptive LoRA specialists (reach/grasp/open/place) — built, trained, and honestly diagnosed: visual phase detection confused drawer-pulls with reaches
  • Also shipped: an auto-porting port-identifier tool for OMX setup (device detection, camera indices, calibration guards) with docs and upstream PR

IGEN — Parent-Child Reinforcement Learning for VLA Fine-Tuning

Pure imitation assumes the robot never makes mistakes. IGEN assumes it will — and learns from exactly those states.

  • A parent module decides where to generate corrective experience; a TD3 child (double Q-functions) learns from it — DAgger-style expert queries without full expert relabeling
  • +10% over BC baselines on LIBERO-Spatial in noise-injected conditions sampled from my own teleop distribution
  • TurboVLA fine-tuning (12 h on A100): 2.8× MAE improvement over 1,250 steps, 6.8× better than predict-mean baseline
  • Insight worth writing down: pure BC wins on clean benchmarks precisely because they contain no bad states — the case for corrective RL is the messy real world

PDPM — Perceptron-Driven Pipeline Morphing ⚙️ systems thread · first-author

A morphable RISC-V processor that reconfigures its pipeline depth at runtime, steered by an online-learned predictor

Pipeline depth is fixed at design time — but the optimal depth changes with the workload, and even between phases of one program. PDPM makes it a runtime variable.

  • Morphable RV32IM superset datapath: 3/5/7-stage modes via bypass multiplexers, with mode-appropriate clock scaling — 12 valid (depth, clock) configurations
  • Key insight: dominated configurations collapse the decision to 3 choices, so a 2-output cycle-ratio regressor (10 int8 weights, ~40 LUTs) replaces a 12-class classifier — and wins on every metric
  • 94.28% ± 2.48% configuration accuracy at +0.107% overhead across 301 benchmarks under nested 5-fold cross-validation; matches a per-phase oracle within +0.1% on six multi-phase workloads
  • ~4,000 lines of Verilog, 15 modules; validated on Lattice iCE40 UP5K (Yosys/nextpnr) and Xilinx Artix-7 (Vivado); predictor adds <1% logic

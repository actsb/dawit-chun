About

From digital logic
to learning systems.

I entered university at fifteen. Three years later, I am the first author of an IEEE ICCD 2026 submission — a RISC-V processor that morphs its pipeline depth at runtime under an online-learned controller, written in ~4,000 lines of Verilog and validated on two FPGAs.

The result I am proudest of is not the accuracy number, but the reformulation behind it: recognizing that twelve legal configurations collapse to three non-dominated choices turned a 60-weight classifier into a 10-weight regressor that is simultaneously more accurate and 6× cheaper in hardware. That is how I like to work — find the structure of the problem, then build the machine that exploits it.

I work fluently across the stack: digital design (Verilog, SystemVerilog, VHDL), FPGA flows (Yosys/nextpnr, Vivado), ML systems (TensorFlow, OpenCV), robotics (ROS), and embedded hardware (PCB design, ESP32, Raspberry Pi). I am preparing to apply for PhD programs in ECE and CS (Fall 2027), aiming to make learning a first-class citizen inside computer architecture.

IEEE ICCD 2026 2026 First author · in submission

PDPM — Perceptron-Driven Pipeline Morphing ⚙️ flagship work

A morphable RISC-V processor that reconfigures its pipeline depth at runtime, steered by an online-learned predictor

Pipeline depth is fixed at design time — but the optimal depth changes with the workload, and even between phases of one program. PDPM makes it a runtime variable.

  • Morphable RV32IM superset datapath: 3/5/7-stage modes via bypass multiplexers, with mode-appropriate clock scaling — 12 valid (depth, clock) configurations
  • Key insight: dominated clock points collapse the decision to 3 choices, so a 2-output cycle-ratio regressor (10 int8 weights, ~40 LUTs) replaces a 12-class classifier — and wins on every metric
  • 94.28% ± 2.48% configuration accuracy at +0.107% overhead across 301 benchmarks under nested 5-fold cross-validation; matches a per-phase oracle within +0.1% on six multi-phase workloads
  • ~4,000 lines of Verilog, 15 modules; validated on Lattice iCE40 UP5K (Yosys/nextpnr) and Xilinx Artix-7 (Vivado); predictor adds <1% logic

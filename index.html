About

Robots that learn,
down to the silicon.

I spend my days teaching robot arms to see, plan, and act. On the Humanoid Software Team at ROBOTIS — makers of the OpenManipulator and DYNAMIXEL — I benchmark 2026's state-of-the-art VLA and world models (GR00T N1.7, MolmoAct2, VLA-JEPA, FastWAM, LingBot-VA) on real manipulators with data I collect myself, and I build new policies when the existing ones fall short: Prior-ACT, a DINOv2 scene-prior imitation model that beats vanilla ACT, and IGEN, a parent-child reinforcement-learning algorithm for VLA fine-tuning.

What makes my approach different is that I go all the way down the stack. When a policy jitters, I write the Savitzky-Golay seam filters and chunk-ensembling logic. When attention drifts off-workspace, I prune image-patch tokens at ≤10 ms latency. And when I want compute itself to adapt, I build the processor: my first-author IEEE ICCD 2026 submission is a morphable RISC-V core steered by an online-learned perceptron — ~4,000 lines of Verilog on two FPGAs. Computer architecture and semiconductor engineering remain my strong second thread.

I am preparing to apply for PhD programs in robotics and ECE/CS (Fall 2027), aiming at physical AI: robot learning that is efficient, adaptive, and honest about its failure modes.

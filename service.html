Taejae University · Fall 2025 & Spring 2026

Teaching Assistant — physics & quantitative thinking

Selected as TA for two courses: NS200 Physics in the Modern Society and XD221 Quantitative Thinking in the Sciences.

  • Led weekly TA sessions; created and graded problem sets and projects
  • Delivered advanced lectures beyond the curriculum — connecting theoretical AI and electronics principles back to the math and physics underneath
  • Learned to explain the same idea five different ways — the exact skill that later made 30 Cambodian teachers' robots actually run
Phnom Penh, Cambodia · KOICA / Korea Productivity Center · July 2022 & July 2023 · 192 hours

STEM Teachers Training — teaching the teachers

For two consecutive summers, I traveled to Phnom Penh as a STEM instructor for 30 Cambodian high-school science teachers, in a program run by KOICA and the Korea Productivity Center: "STEM Training Practice for Physics Using Robots and STEM Education."

  • What I taught: Arduino programming and wiring, 3D modeling, and C programming — hands-on at the workbench, one debugging session at a time
  • What we built: a basketball-shooting robot (2022) and a line-tracing sumo robot (2023), with step-by-step materials the teachers could rebuild with their own students
  • What happened next: teachers adapted our materials in their own classrooms, and the program spread to four additional provinces

When a robot fails in front of 30 teachers and a translator, you learn very quickly what you actually understand.

Geumcheon-gu, Seoul · 2021 – 2022 · 42 hours classroom support

Geumcheon science classrooms — where I started

Geumcheon's science-festival ecosystem is where I built my first robots — and where I first went back to help the next group of kids build theirs.

  • Science classroom support (42 h): assisted elementary science classes — kit assembly, wiring fixes, and first debugging sessions
  • Competition roots: my first hackathon win was the Geumcheon Hackathon (1st Place, 2022, age 14), in the same community that hosted my earliest robot contests
Gangnam-gu urban wetlands, Seoul · 2023 – 2025 · 69 hours

Amphibian conservation — field data for the city

Night surveys of endangered frog and salamander populations in urban wetlands — 69 hours of fieldwork feeding municipal conservation records.

  • GPS-tagged observations and environmental documentation, survey after survey
  • Fieldwork with my sister and fellow volunteers — the least glamorous data collection I have ever done, and some of the most careful
